The kitchen renovation process in Mandurah

In short: A kitchen renovation runs from brief and design to approvals, demolition, rough-in, installation and handover. In Western Australia, home building contracts between $7,500 and $500,000 are covered by the Home Building Contracts Act, and residential building work over $20,000 needs home indemnity insurance before any payment.

A step-by-step guide with the official rules for your state. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This guide explains a typical kitchen renovation and the official rules that apply in Mandurah. It is not legal advice. Follow the links, and ask the renovator to confirm what's needed for your home.

1. Brief and budget

Decide what's wrong with the current kitchen, what you want to keep, and your budget, including a contingency. Collect photos of kitchens you like.

2. Measure, design and quote

The renovator measures up, talks through layout and materials, and prepares a design and a written quote. Check what's included: demolition, plumbing, electrical, splashback, flooring, painting, appliances and rubbish removal.

3. Approvals and contract

In Western Australia, a building permit is generally needed if you are constructing, altering or extending a building, including moving or removing structural walls. Like-for-like cabinet work may not need a permit, but plumbing and electrical work must be done by licensed trades. If the job is residential building work over $20,000, home indemnity insurance and, in the prescribed area, a registered building contractor will usually apply, and the Home Building Contracts Act covers many contracts between $7,500 and $500,000. Ask the City of Mandurah or a building surveyor whether a permit is required for your renovation.

Sources: Building and Energy, Building or renovating your home; Building and Energy, Do I need to be a registered building contractor?.

In Western Australia, the Home Building Contracts Act 1991 generally applies to fixed-price home building or associated work contracts between $7,500 and $500,000. For residential building work valued at more than $20,000, a registered builder must take out home indemnity insurance in the owner's name and give you the certificate before taking any payment or starting work. A registered building contractor is required when the work needs a building permit, is valued at $20,000 or more (or $50,000 or more for a Class 10a building such as a shed or garage), and is in the prescribed area of the State, unless an exemption applies. You can check a builder's registration with Building and Energy.

Sources: Building and Energy, Building or renovating your home; Building and Energy, Do I need to be a registered building contractor?; Building and Energy, Home indemnity insurance fact sheet.

4. Demolition and rough-in

The old kitchen comes out, then the plumber and electrician run new services before walls are patched. Homes built before about 1990 may contain asbestos, which must be handled safely by people qualified to do it.

5. Installation and finishing

Cabinets, benchtops, splashback, appliances, tapware and lighting go in, followed by final connections and finishing. Expect to be without a working kitchen for part of the job, so ask for a timeline.

6. Handover

Walk through the finished kitchen with the renovator, note any defects in writing, and keep the compliance certificates for plumbing and electrical work and any warranty documents.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources before relying on this summary.
